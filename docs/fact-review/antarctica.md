## Wording changed because of what the lookups showed

- **Lambert Glacier.** Wikipedia says "the world's largest glacier", but search results show that depends on how you measure and on counting the Amery Ice Shelf with it. The record instead credits Guinness for "longest", and gives no length: the figures run from 435 km (glacier alone) to 1,470 km (Guinness flow line).
- **Cape Denison.** Popular lists give an average of about 80 km/h, and the official site says 71 km/h as an annual average daily maximum. No figure is used. Guinness-style lists credit Commonwealth Bay, the bay around the cape. "Known as" is deliberate, and "at sea level" comes from the official site.
- **Mount Erebus.** Height is left out: Wikipedia says 3,792 m and NASA says 3,794 m.
- **Lake Vostok.** Area is left out: 12,500 km² on Wikipedia, 14,000 km² from Guinness. "Largest known" is used, because new lakes are still being mapped under the ice.
- **Lake Vostok drilling date.** The year only, since sources give 5 or 6 February 2012.
- **Deception Island.** The Treaty plan calls it the "only place in the world" for sailing into a restless caldera, and also claims the world's largest chinstrap colony. Neither is used. The chinstrap claim was not checked. The copy read is an older version of the plan; a revision was adopted in 2019 (Measure 10) and was not read.
- **Mount Vinson.** First ascent (1966, per Wikipedia) was not used for a question. I recall the same team climbing other peaks that trip, which would give a 1966 question several answers, but I did not look that up.
- **Name notes added:** South Pole against South Magnetic Pole, Mount Vinson against Vinson Massif, Lake Vostok against Vostok Station, Cape Denison against Mawson Station, and where Deception Island's name comes from.

## Relied on general knowledge, not looked up

- McMurdo Station is American and "big" (hint2 for Mount Erebus). Wikipedia confirmed it is a US station near Erebus; "big" was not looked up.
- "Snowy" for Erebus and "strip of mountains" for the Peninsula.
- "Still very cold" for the Peninsula. Wikipedia says the climate is mild only by Antarctic standards.
- Lake Vostok and Lambert Glacier being in East Antarctica. Both follow from their coordinates, but the phrase itself was not checked.

## Could not confirm, or for a parent to decide

- **Britannica** returned 403 for the Ross Ice Shelf and South Pole pages, so those rest on the sources above. The SCAR gazetteer entry for Lambert Glacier also failed (redirect, then 403), so its lonLat of 71°S 70°E comes from Wikipedia alone. That point is near the glacier's lower end, not its middle.
- **Vinson height.** 4,892 m is the standard figure, but Live Science and 7summits.com appeared in search results with 4,897 m. Live Science could not be read in full.
- **"The Barrier"** rests on Wikipedia alone.
- **Amery Ice Shelf.** Other, smaller glaciers also reach it, so the question says "main river of ice". That Lambert is the main one is supported by Guinness and by the glaciology search results, not stated in those exact words.
- **Peninsula against Deception Island.** The South Shetland Islands lie slightly closer to South America, so both Peninsula questions say "mainland".
- **Guinness.** Its clearest-sea page shows a "When" of 2004 although the measurement was 1986. A Polar Record note gives 79 m rather than 80 m.
- **Lake Vostok.** Life in the lake is not mentioned. I did not research it.
- **Territorial claims.** Nothing here says who claims what, and this was not researched beyond Wikipedia placing Erebus in the New Zealand claim.
