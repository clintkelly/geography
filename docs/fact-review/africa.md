# Fact review: Africa

Checked 2026-11-01. Covers `src/content/world/africa.json`: 54 records (all UN member states in Africa; Western Sahara and Somaliland skipped), 108 facts and 108 questions.

**How far this goes.** "Looked up" below means a web search or page fetch was run for that claim. Most lookups were read through search summaries, not by opening each page in full, and some sources are secondary (Wikipedia, news, travel sites). The URL given is one the lookup actually returned. Anything not in the table is general knowledge only: reviewed, not sourced.

## For the owner to decide

1. **Equatorial Guinea.** `capital` is Ciudad de la Paz, which is legally right but barely built. Malabo is in the city list, so a child who answers Malabo from an older atlas is marked wrong. Older atlases and printed maps will disagree.
2. **Egypt.** Cairo kept. This may need changing if The New Capital gets formal status.
3. **South Africa, Benin, Burundi, Eswatini, Côte d'Ivoire and Tanzania.** Each has a real split between official capital and seat of government or largest city. Cotonou, Bujumbura, Abidjan and Dar es Salaam appear as wrong answers to the capital question. That is correct, but it is a hard question for this age.
4. **Djibouti.** The capital is stored as "Djibouti City" so it doesn't match the country's name in choice lists. It gets a `hint2`, not `cityState`.
5. **`hint2` beyond the rule.** Added for Algeria, Tunisia, Guinea-Bissau and São Tomé and Príncipe, whose capitals (Algiers, Tunis, Bissau, São Tomé) overlap the country's name closely enough to give it away.
6. **Morocco and Mauritania** are linked although they don't share a recognized border; Western Sahara lies between them and was skipped. The other non-land links are Cabo Verde to Senegal; Comoros to Madagascar and Tanzania; Madagascar to Mozambique and Mauritius; Mauritius to Seychelles; Seychelles to Kenya; and São Tomé and Príncipe to Gabon and Equatorial Guinea.
7. **Somalia.** The fact about its coastline counts the coast of Somaliland. The city list avoids Somaliland cities. The fact itself says nothing about the dispute.
8. **Facts with a hard history behind them.** No record states it, but a parent may want to know: Ganvié was founded by people escaping slave raids; Dahomey's kingdom took part in the slave trade; Liberia's naming ties to the American settlement of freed slaves; Eritrea's camel comes from its independence war. Sudan and South Sudan are at war or unstable now, and their records are neutral.
9. **Tiny.** Set for Cabo Verde, Comoros, Gambia, Mauritius, São Tomé and Príncipe and Seychelles, using the game's world projection: everything smaller than Montenegro, the smallest non-tiny Europe country in the set measured. eSwatini, Djibouti and Rwanda are just above that line and might be hard to click.
10. **Somalia's camel table and the coastline figure** are older data. The ranking is unlikely to change, but it is not recent.
