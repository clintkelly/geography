## Could not confirm, or for a parent to decide

- **Florida "Sunshine State".** Several secondary sources say the Legislature adopted it in 1970, but it is not in the Florida Statutes and no state source could be opened. It is probably real; a state page would justify setting it back to `official`.
- **Statute text.** Arkansas, Hawaii, Arizona and California were read through legal mirrors rather than the state's own site.
- **Statue of Liberty.** The island's state is legally odd: it sits in New Jersey waters, is usually described as an exclave of New York, and sources differ on what the 1834 compact and the 1998 Ellis Island case mean for it. The question was rewritten to avoid the issue.
- **Hells Canyon.** The US Forest Service page could not be opened, and rival claims (Copper Canyon, Kings Canyon) could not be settled, so "deepest in North America" was dropped.
- **Tara Canyon.** Some sources call it the deepest in continental Europe, depending on where Europe ends. "One of the deepest" is true either way.
- **Lake counts** depend on the size cutoff. A database that counts only lakes over 0.1 square km gives Belarus 1,420.
- **Close population races** that could flip with the next estimates: Charleston and Huntington WV (46,117 to 44,815), Charleston and Columbia SC, Bridgeport and Stamford CT, Cheyenne and Casper WY.
- **Largest cities outside the US** are from general knowledge except the four tiny countries above. Belgium counts Antwerp as largest by city limits; greater Brussels is bigger.
- **Gulf of Mexico.** Owner decision: the game says Gulf of Mexico.
- **Bern** is Switzerland's seat of government, but Swiss law does not formally name a capital (general knowledge, not looked up).
- **Moldova's language.** Romanian and Moldovan are the same language, but people label it differently and the topic is political.
- **Kosovo, Cyprus and Turkey** are not in the game, so neighbor lists for Serbia, Albania, Montenegro and North Macedonia leave out Kosovo.
- **Monaco and San Marino** "cities" are quarters and municipalities, not separate cities.

## Wave 3: 38 creatures and three Wild Paths lines (2026-11-06)

Written from memory with Wikipedia sources (on each fact in `src/content/guide/creatures.json`), not yet checked against the pages. Flavor lines are in `src/content/pack.ts`.

- **hornsquirt (Texas Horned Lizard, us-south):** When in danger, it can squirt blood from the corners of its eyes to scare off predators. It eats mostly harvester ants. It is the state reptile of Texas. Size: About 7 to 11 cm (3 to 4.5 inches) long.
- **rackit (Raccoon, us-south):** Raccoons have very sensitive front paws and are famous for opening latches and lids. Scientists have found raccoons can remember how to solve a task for up to three years. The black fur around its eyes looks like a bandit's mask. Size: About 40 to 70 cm (16 to 28 inches) long, plus its ringed tail.
- **chipdig (Eastern Chipmunk, us-northeast):** Chipmunks carry food in stretchy cheek pouches. It stores seeds and nuts in its burrow to eat through the winter. Its burrows can be more than 3 m (10 feet) long. Size: About 25 cm (10 inches) long, including its tail.
- **quillsteel (North American Porcupine, us-northeast):** It has about 30,000 quills. Porcupines can't throw their quills, but the quills come off easily when touched. It is a good climber and eats bark, leaves and twigs. Size: About 60 to 90 cm (2 to 3 feet) long.
- **crestalon (Bald Eagle, us-northeast):** It is the national bird of the United States. It builds the biggest nest of any North American bird. Bald eagles almost disappeared from the lower 48 states but made a big comeback. Size: Wings about 1.8 to 2.3 m (6 to 7.5 feet) across.
- **leapcroc (Cuban Crocodile, america-caribbean):** It lives only in Cuba, mostly in the Zapata Swamp. It is known for leaping up out of the water. Its scales have bright yellow-and-black speckles. Size: Usually about 2 to 2.5 m (6.5 to 8 feet) long.
- **digdillo (Giant Armadillo, south-america):** It is the largest living armadillo. The third claw on each front foot is the longest claw of any living mammal, compared to body size. It digs big burrows that many other animals use too. Size: About 75 to 100 cm (2.5 to 3.3 feet) long, plus its tail.
- **macawsong (Hyacinth Macaw, south-america):** It is the longest parrot in the world, and the largest flying parrot. Its powerful beak can crack hard palm nuts. It has bright yellow skin around its eyes and at the base of its beak. Size: About 1 m (3.3 feet) long, the longest parrot.
- **dartblaze (Poison Dart Frog, south-america):** Their bright colors warn predators that they are poisonous. Many kinds are less than 2.5 cm (1 inch) long. In zoos, they usually lose their poison, because it comes from the bugs they eat in the wild. Size: Most are about 1.5 to 6 cm (under 2.5 inches) long.
- **stiltwolf (Maned Wolf, south-america):** It is the tallest wild dog, but it isn't a true wolf or fox. Its long legs help it see over tall grass. It eats a lot of fruit, especially one called the wolf apple. Size: About 90 cm (3 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **strapguin (Chinstrap Penguin, antarctica):** The thin black line under its chin looks like the strap of a helmet. It is one of the most common penguins, with millions of breeding pairs. Chinstrap penguins take thousands of very short naps a day. Size: About 70 cm (2.3 feet) tall.
- **trunkseal (Southern Elephant Seal, antarctica):** It is the largest seal in the world. Males use their big trunk-like noses to make loud roaring sounds. It can dive more than 1,500 m (5,000 feet) deep. Size: Males up to about 5 to 6 m (16 to 20 feet) long.
- **barraclad (Great Barracuda, america-caribbean):** Barracudas are fast hunters that dash forward in short bursts. They have long fang-like teeth and a big underbite. Young barracudas often hide among seagrass and mangroves. Size: Commonly up to about 1.5 m (5 feet) long.
- **toothfrost (Antarctic Toothfish, antarctica):** It has antifreeze proteins in its blood so it doesn't freeze. It is one of the largest fish in the Southern Ocean. It can live for about 50 years. Size: Up to about 1.7 m (5.5 feet) long.
- **gobblaze (Wild Turkey, us-south):** Male wild turkeys gobble loudly and fan their tails to show off. Unlike farm turkeys, wild turkeys can fly short distances. Wild turkeys almost disappeared in the 1900s, then made a big comeback. Size: About 1 to 1.2 m (3.3 to 4 feet) long.
- **bluepinch (Atlantic Blue Crab, us-south):** Its back legs are shaped like paddles, so it is a strong swimmer. Its scientific name means 'beautiful savory swimmer'. It is famous in the Chesapeake Bay. Size: Shell about 23 cm (9 inches) wide.
- **snowshoe (Snowshoe Hare, us-northeast):** Its fur turns white in winter and brown in summer. Its big furry hind feet keep it from sinking into snow. Its numbers rise and fall in a roughly 10-year cycle, along with the lynx that hunt it. Size: About 40 to 50 cm (16 to 20 inches) long.
- **badgerock (European Badger, europe-west):** Badgers live in big underground homes called setts. Some setts are used by badger families for many generations. It has a bold black-and-white striped face. Size: About 60 to 90 cm (2 to 3 feet) long.
- **squirrust (Red Squirrel, europe-west):** It grows long ear tufts in winter. It buries food to eat later and doesn't find all of it, which helps new trees grow. In Britain it has become rare where gray squirrels from North America moved in. Size: About 19 to 23 cm (7.5 to 9 inches) long, plus its tail.
- **leapsalmon (Atlantic Salmon, europe-west):** Salmon hatch in rivers, grow up at sea, then return to rivers to lay eggs. They can leap up waterfalls on their way upstream. Unlike Pacific salmon, an Atlantic salmon can make the trip more than once. Size: Usually about 70 to 75 cm (28 to 30 inches) long.
- **sturgeonplate (Beluga Sturgeon, europe-east):** It is one of the biggest freshwater fish in the world. Sturgeons have rows of bony plates called scutes instead of scales. Beluga sturgeons can live for more than 100 years. Size: Can grow longer than 6 m (20 feet).
- **bustarboom (Great Bustard, europe-east):** Males are among the heaviest flying birds in the world. In spring, males puff up into a big white ball of feathers to impress females. Males grow long whisker-like feathers on their chins. Size: Males up to about 1 m (3.3 feet) long.
- **molediggs (European Mole, europe-east):** It digs tunnels with its wide, shovel-like front paws. Its velvety fur can lie in any direction, so it can move backward in tunnels easily. Moles store worms in underground larders. Size: About 15 cm (6 inches) long.
- **lynxshade (Iberian Lynx, europe-south):** It was once the most endangered cat in the world, and conservation work has helped it recover. It eats mostly European rabbits. It has black ear tufts and a beard-like ruff of fur on its face. Size: About 85 to 110 cm (2.8 to 3.6 feet) long.
- **sealmonk (Mediterranean Monk Seal, europe-south):** It is one of the rarest seals in the world. It often rests and raises its pups in sea caves. Its numbers have been slowly growing thanks to protection. Size: About 2.4 m (8 feet) long.
- **coilball (Ball Python, africa-west):** It curls into a tight ball with its head in the middle when it feels threatened. It is a constrictor, not a venomous snake. It is also called the royal python. Size: Usually about 1 to 1.5 m (3 to 5 feet) long.
- **hammerkop (Hammerkop, africa-west):** Its crest and beak together make its head look like a hammer. It builds huge dome nests that can be more than 1.5 m (5 feet) across. In many African stories, the hammerkop is a magical or unlucky bird. Size: About 56 cm (22 inches) long.
- **goldcatshade (African Golden Cat, africa-central):** It is one of the least-known wild cats, rarely seen by people. Its fur can be golden-red, brown or gray. Its closest relative is the caracal. Size: About 60 to 100 cm (2 to 3.3 feet) long.
- **goliathorn (Goliath Beetle, africa-central):** Goliath beetles are among the heaviest insects in the world. Males have a Y-shaped horn on their head. Their grubs can weigh up to about 100 grams. Size: Males up to about 11 cm (4.3 inches) long.
- **honeyfu (Honey Badger, africa-south):** It is famous for being fearless and will stand up to lions. Its thick, loose skin lets it twist around to bite back if grabbed. It loves honey and bee grubs and digs into beehives. Size: About 55 to 77 cm (22 to 30 inches) long.
- **kickretary (Secretary Bird, africa-south):** It hunts on foot, stomping on snakes and lizards with very fast kicks. Its crest of long black feathers may have reminded people of quill pens tucked behind a clerk's ear. It is on the coat of arms of South Africa. Size: About 1.3 m (4.3 feet) tall.

Grown-up moths do not eat, and they live for less than a week. Females give off a scent chemical called a pheromone, and males pick it up with special sensors on their antennae. Size: Wings about 15 cm (6 inches) across.
- **magnetle (Hercules beetle, Science Egg):** It is the longest kind of beetle alive today. Males fight by grabbing a rival between their horns, then lifting and throwing it. Scientists think the color of its wing covers can be affected by how humid the place it grows up is. Size: Males up to about 17 cm (almost 7 inches) long, including the horn.
- **crystalisk (Thorny devil, Science Egg):** Ridges on its scales collect water, and capillary action carries the water through channels in its skin to its mouth. It has a spiny bump on its neck called a false head, which it shows to predators by tucking its real head down. It often eats thousands of ants in one day. Size: Up to about 21 cm (8 inches) long, including its tail.
- **phasepup (Arctic fox, Science Egg):** Its fur keeps heat in better than the fur of any other mammal. It sheds twice a year, from a light winter coat to a darker summer coat. Its round body shape helps it lose less heat. Size: About 46 to 68 cm (18 to 27 inches) long, plus a bushy tail.
- **gravitail (Eastern gray squirrel, Science Egg):** It hides food in lots of small hiding spots to dig up later. It is one of the most important animals for helping forests grow back in eastern North America. If it thinks it is being watched, it may pretend to bury a nut to trick the watcher. Size: Body about 23 to 30 cm (9 to 12 inches) long, plus a 19 to 25 cm (7.5 to 10 inch) tail.
- **gyrosnail (Garden snail, Science Egg):** In dry or cold weather, it seals its shell shut with a thin layer of dried slime. Each snail has both male and female parts. A popular estimate says its top speed is about 1.3 cm (half an inch) per second. Size: Shell about 2.5 to 4 cm (1 to 1.6 inches) across.
- **lensowl (Northern saw-whet owl, Science Egg):** Its name comes from its call, which some people say sounds like a saw being sharpened. An owl's eyes are shaped like tubes and are fixed in place, so it turns its whole head to look around. Owls can turn their heads as much as 270 degrees. Size: About 17 to 22 cm (7 to 8.5 inches) long.
- **metrodillo (Nine-banded armadillo, Science Egg):** It almost always has four identical babies, because one early embryo splits into four. When frightened, it can jump 91 to 120 cm (3 to 4 feet) straight up in the air. It can hold its breath for up to six minutes and walk across the bottom of a river. Size: About 64 to 107 cm (25 to 42 inches) long, including its tail.
- **querifox (Red fox, Science Egg):** It lives in more places around the world than any other meat-eating mammal. It hunts small animals hidden under snow or in tall grass. Scientists think it lines up with Earth's magnetic field to help it hunt under snow. Size: Body about 45 to 90 cm (18 to 35 inches) long, plus a bushy tail.
- **solubee (Western honey bee, Science Egg):** Worker bees turn nectar into honey by drying out its extra water. Bees share news with each other using scent chemicals and the waggle dance. A colony has one queen, many female workers and a few male drones. Size: Workers are a little over 1 cm (about half an inch) long.
- **boomardier (Bombardier beetle, Science Egg):** Its spray comes from a chemical reaction between two chemicals, hydroquinone and hydrogen peroxide. The reaction heats the spray to near the boiling point of water. Some bombardier beetles can point their spray in many different directions. Size: Most are small, about 1 to 2 cm (under 1 inch) long.

## Wave 6: animals for the thinnest regions, and Winter Festival creatures (2026-11-08)

Each fact was checked against the cited Wikipedia page by the writing agent; a parent should still skim them.

- **addune (Addax):** Its long horns twist in one and a half to three spirals. It can live without drinking water for a long time, getting moisture from the plants it eats. Its wide, flat hooves help it walk on soft sand. Size: About 1 to 1.1 m (3.3 to 3.6 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **jerbounce (Lesser Egyptian jerboa):** It hops on its long back legs, like a tiny kangaroo. It is nocturnal and spends hot days in its burrow. It can leap up to 3 m (10 feet) in a single bound. Size: Body about 10 cm (4 inches) long, plus a longer tail.
- **hornwinder (Saharan horned viper):** Many have a small horn above each eye. It can move by sidewinding across loose sand. It hunts by lying buried in the sand, waiting for prey to come close. Size: Usually about 30 to 60 cm (1 to 2 feet) long.
- **pebblebeak (Egyptian vulture):** It uses pebbles as tools, throwing them to break open ostrich eggs. Its face has bare skin that is bright yellow. In ancient Egypt, its picture was used as a hieroglyph. Size: About 47 to 65 cm (1.5 to 2 feet) long.
- **dunedive (Sandfish skink):** It moves through sand by wiggling its body, almost like swimming. Its smooth, shiny scales help it slide through sand. Its wedge-shaped snout helps it push into the sand. Size: Up to about 20 cm (8 inches) long.
- **lynxtuft (Eurasian lynx):** It is the largest kind of lynx. It has black tufts of fur on the tips of its ears. Its big furry paws work like snowshoes. Size: Body about 73 to 106 cm (2.4 to 3.5 feet) long, plus a short tail.
- **burrowpeek (European ground squirrel):** It lives in groups and digs burrows in short grass. It hibernates through the winter. It often sits up to look for danger, and whistles loudly when alarmed. Size: About 20 cm (8 inches) long, plus a short tail.
- **bristleboar (Wild boar):** The wild boar is the wild ancestor of the farm pig. Piglets have light stripes along their backs. In males, the big canine teeth, or tusks, keep growing all their lives. Size: In Europe, usually about 1.4 to 1.6 m (4.5 to 5.2 feet) long.
- **bellyblaze (European fire-bellied toad):** Its belly is red with thick black blotches. When threatened, it lifts its arms or even flips over to flash its red belly as a warning. It is known for its soft "whoop" call. Size: Up to about 5.6 cm (2.2 inches) long.
- **hoopcrest (Eurasian hoopoe):** It has a crest of feathers that it can raise like a fan. Its call is a soft "oop-oop-oop". It is the national bird of Israel. Size: About 25 to 32 cm (10 to 12.5 inches) long.
- **adeliebop (Adelie penguin):** It has a white ring around each eye. It builds its nest out of small stones. It was named after the wife of a French explorer, Adele. Size: About 70 to 73 cm (28 inches) long.
- **gentoozoom (Gentoo penguin):** It is the fastest underwater swimmer of all penguins. It has a bright orange-red bill and a white stripe across the top of its head. It has the most noticeable tail of any penguin. Size: About 70 to 90 cm (28 to 35 inches) long.
- **furflipper (Antarctic fur seal):** About 95% of them live on the island of South Georgia. Its outer ears are long and easy to see. Males are much bigger than females. Size: Males up to about 2 m (6.5 feet) long; females about 1.4 m (4.6 feet).
- **skuastorm (South polar skua):** It breeds on the coasts of Antarctica. It has been seen at the actual South Pole. It swoops at the heads of people who come near its nest. Size: About 53 cm (21 inches) long.
- **spindlesea (Giant Antarctic sea spider):** It breathes by soaking up oxygen through its skin. Its gut stretches all the way into its legs. It catches jellyfish and other drifting animals with its long snout. Size: Leg span up to about 70 cm (2.3 feet).
- **buzzbeater (European bee-eater):** It eats insects, especially bees, wasps and hornets. It hits a caught bee against a perch to remove the stinger. It nests in tunnels it digs in sandy banks. Size: About 27 to 29 cm (11 inches) long, including tail streamers.
- **olivetort (Hermann's tortoise):** Its shell has a yellow and black pattern. It sniffs to decide which plants to eat. It spends the winter hibernating in a bed of dead leaves. Size: The eastern kind grows up to about 28 cm (11 inches) long; the western kind is smaller.
- **quillmane (Crested porcupine):** It is one of the largest rodents in the world. When disturbed, it raises and fans its quills to look bigger, and its hollow tail quills rattle. It is active mostly at night. Size: About 60 to 83 cm (2 to 2.7 feet) long, not counting the tail.
- **imperagle (Spanish imperial eagle):** It lives only in Spain and Portugal. Adults have white patches on their shoulders. It eats mostly rabbits. Size: Wings about 1.8 to 2.1 m (6 to 7 feet) across.
- **genettle (Common genet):** It has rows of black spots, and its long tail has 8 to 13 dark rings. Adults are active mostly at night, especially just after sunset. People brought it to Europe from North Africa about 1,000 to 1,500 years ago. Size: Body about 43 to 55 cm (17 to 22 inches) long, plus a tail almost as long.
- **grovephant (African forest elephant):** It is smaller than the African bush elephant. Its tusks are straight and point down. It spreads the seeds of many rainforest trees. Size: On average about 2.16 m (7 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **bongobeat (Bongo):** It has a red-brown coat with thin white stripes. Both males and females have horns, but males have bigger ones. It is one of the largest forest antelopes. Size: About 1.1 to 1.3 m (3.6 to 4.3 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **tigerfang (Goliath tigerfish):** It lives in the Congo River. Its big teeth are spaced out and shaped like daggers. It is the largest kind of tigerfish. Size: Often about 1.5 m (5 feet) long; the biggest reach about 2 m (6.6 feet).
- **zapnose (Peters's elephantnose fish):** It makes weak electric fields to find food and get around. Its trunk is really part of its mouth, used to find worms and insects. Its brain uses a bigger share of its oxygen than any other known animal with a backbone. Size: Usually about 23 to 25 cm (9 to 10 inches) long.
- **turquaco (Great blue turaco):** It is the largest turaco. It has a tall black crest on its head. It eats mostly fruit and leaves. Size: About 70 to 76 cm (2.3 to 2.5 feet) long.
- **stripyena (Striped hyena):** It has a long mane of tough hair along its back. It comes out only in full darkness and goes back to its den before sunrise. It is the national animal of Lebanon. Size: About 85 to 130 cm (2.8 to 4.3 feet) long, plus its tail.
- **nubiclimb (Nubian ibex):** Its long horns curve up, then back and down. It stays close to cliffs, where it can escape from predators. Males grow a beard that gets longer and darker as they age. Size: About 65 to 75 cm (2.1 to 2.5 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **baskspike (Egyptian spiny-tailed lizard):** It can swing its heavy, spiky tail at an attacker. Spiny-tailed lizards eat mostly plants. It sleeps in its burrow with its spiky tail near the door to block intruders. Size: Males average about 76 cm (2.5 feet) long, including the tail.
- **hyrockus (Rock hyrax):** It looks a bit like a marmot, but it is much more closely related to elephants and manatees. It hides from predators in cracks between rocks. It lives in groups of about 10 to 80 animals. Size: Up to about 50 cm (1.6 feet) long.
- **goldnibble (Golden hamster (Syrian hamster)):** In the wild, it lives in Syria and Turkey. It carries food in big cheek pouches. Golden hamsters bred by people are popular pets. Size: Up to about 18 cm (7 inches) long.
- **royalette (Royal antelope):** It is the smallest antelope in the world. It can cover 2.8 m (9 feet) in a single leap. It is shy and active mostly at night. Size: About 25 cm (10 inches) tall at the shoulder.
- **manatide (African manatee):** It is the only kind of manatee that lives outside the Americas. It eats mostly plants. Its gut can be 20 m (66 feet) long, which helps it digest all those plants. Size: Up to about 4.5 m (15 feet) long.
- **scorpglow (Emperor scorpion):** It is one of the largest scorpions in the world. Like other scorpions, it glows under ultraviolet light. It prefers to use its big pincers to crush its prey. Size: About 20 cm (8 inches) long.
- **crowncrane (Black crowned crane):** It has a crown of stiff golden feathers on its head. It is the national bird of Nigeria. Its call is a low, mellow honk. Size: Up to about 105 cm (3.4 feet) long.
- **hairyhop (Hairy frog):** Breeding males grow hair-like strands of skin on their sides and thighs. The strands help the frog take in more oxygen. It can push sharp claws out through the skin of its back toes. Size: About 10 to 13 cm (4 to 5 inches) long.
- **riverotto (Eurasian otter):** It eats mostly fish. It is the most widespread kind of otter in the world. It marks its territory with its droppings, which are called spraints. Size: About 57 to 95 cm (2 to 3 feet) long, plus its tail.
- **kingflash (Common kingfisher):** It catches fish by diving, and its eyes are specially suited to seeing under water. It nests at the end of a burrow it digs in a riverbank. When it flies away, you can see an electric-blue flash down its back. Size: About 16 cm (6 inches) long.
- **marmotoot (Alpine marmot):** It whistles loudly to warn others when it sees danger. It can spend up to nine months a year hibernating. It lives in the Alps and other high mountains. Size: About 43 to 73 cm (17 to 29 inches) long, plus a 13 to 20 cm tail.
- **dozymouse (Hazel dormouse):** It hibernates from about October to April or May. It has golden-brown fur and big black eyes. It eats hazelnuts, flowers, fruit and insects. Size: Body about 10 cm (4 inches) long; about 16 cm (6 inches) with its tail.
- **cuttleshine (Common cuttlefish):** It can change its color and pattern very quickly. It can even change the bumpy texture of its skin. When startled, it jets away and squirts ink. Size: Body (mantle) up to about 45 cm (18 inches) long.
- **skyraffe (Southern giraffe):** Giraffes are the tallest land animals. They eat leaves from tall trees, especially acacias. Their horn-like bumps are called ossicones. Size: Grown giraffes stand about 4.3 to 5.7 m (14 to 19 feet) tall.
- **hippoplash (Common hippopotamus):** It spends the day in water and comes out at night to eat grass. Its closest living relatives are whales and dolphins. After elephants and rhinos, it is the biggest land mammal. Size: About 3.3 to 3.45 m (11 feet) long, not counting the tail.
- **hooklip (Black rhinoceros):** It has a pointed upper lip for grabbing leaves and twigs. It has two horns on its nose. Despite its name, its color ranges from brown to grey. Size: About 1.3 to 1.8 m (4.3 to 6 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **tuskwart (Common warthog):** When feeding, it often bends its front legs and moves around on its padded wrists. It backs into its burrow so its tusks face out. When threatened, it can run up to 48 km/h (30 mph) with its tail sticking up. Size: About 0.9 to 1.5 m (3 to 5 feet) long.
- **curlspike (Armadillo girdled lizard):** When scared, it grabs its tail in its mouth and rolls into a ball. It lives in the deserts of South Africa. It lives in groups of up to 30 to 60 lizards. Size: Up to about 20 cm (8 inches) long.
- **harpfrost (Harp seal, Winter Festival):** A harp seal pup has a white coat for its first 2 to 3 weeks. Adults have a dark marking on their back shaped like a harp. Every year, harp seals gather on the sea ice to have their pups. Size: Grown-ups about 1.7 to 2 m (5.6 to 6.6 feet) long.
- **frostbun (Arctic hare, Winter Festival):** In the far north, it stays white all year. Its thick fur keeps it warm in the Arctic. It digs holes in the snow to keep warm and to sleep. Size: About 43 to 70 cm (17 to 28 inches) long, plus a short tail.
- **ptarmiflake (Rock ptarmigan, Winter Festival):** Its feathers turn white in winter to match the snow. Its scientific name, Lagopus, means "hare foot", because its legs are feathery. It is the official bird of Nunavut in Canada. Size: About 34 to 36 cm (13 to 14 inches) long.
- **robinglow (European robin, Winter Festival):** It has an orange-red face and chest. Both males and females sing all year, even in winter. In the 1960s, it was picked as the unofficial national bird of the United Kingdom. Size: About 12.5 to 14 cm (5 inches) long.
- **waxtwinkle (Bohemian waxwing, Winter Festival):** Some of its wing feathers have bright red, waxy-looking tips. In winter, rowan berries are one of its favorite foods. In some years, huge numbers fly far beyond their usual winter range looking for fruit. Size: About 19 to 23 cm (7.5 to 9 inches) long.
- **cardinflake (Northern cardinal, Winter Festival):** Males are bright red; females are mostly tan with red tints. It is the state bird of seven US states, including Illinois, Ohio and Virginia. In winter, it fluffs up its feathers to trap warm air next to its body. Size: About 21 to 23.5 cm (8 to 9 inches) long.
- **auroralynx (Canada lynx, Winter Festival):** It depends mostly on snowshoe hares for food. Its big furry paws spread wide to move easily on soft snow. It has black tufts on its ears. Size: About 73 to 107 cm (2.4 to 3.5 feet) long in all.
- **snowbeard (Mountain goat, Winter Festival):** Despite its name, it is not a true goat. Both males and females have beards and short black horns. A woolly greyish-white double coat protects it from the weather. Size: Males stand about 1 m (3.3 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **yakmuffle (Wild yak, Winter Festival):** Its shaggy coat can form a long skirt that reaches the ground. It lives high on the cold Tibetan Plateau. It is the ancestor of the farm yak. Size: About 1.6 to 2 m (5.2 to 6.7 feet) tall at the shoulder.
- **flurryglide (Siberian flying squirrel, Winter Festival):** It glides from tree to tree using flaps of skin between its legs. Its eyes are large and strikingly black. It is the only flying squirrel in Europe. Size: Body about 13 to 20 cm (5 to 8 inches) long, plus a flat tail.
